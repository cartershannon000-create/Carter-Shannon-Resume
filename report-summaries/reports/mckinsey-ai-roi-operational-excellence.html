Putting AI to Work: The Operational Excellence Imperative

report · 2026-07-26 · 11 min read
mckinseyaioperationsoperational-excellenceagentic-aiproductivity

TL;DR

A McKinsey survey of 1,000 executives finds that AI pays off only when it moves beyond pilots and is embedded across the enterprise, and that operational excellence is the hidden accelerator that makes it stick. Companies that combine scaled AI with mature operating disciplines generate roughly double the profit margins and more than five times the three-year ROIC of peers. The takeaway: use operational excellence as the stabilizer and technology as the accelerant to build a durable performance loop.

Key points

  • AI conviction is near-universal but scaling is rare: almost 90% of organizations are at least experimenting with AI, yet only 7% have scaled it across the enterprise.

    Agentic AI is even less penetrated — at most a quarter of organizations are experimenting with AI agents in any function.

  • Enterprise-wide AI adoption is tied to dramatically better financials: nearly double the profit margins and more than 5x the three-year ROIC versus peers using AI in only a few departments.

    The survey shows correlation, not causation, but the pattern across deployment, operations, productivity and returns is highly consistent (Exhibit 1).

  • Operational excellence delivers a parallel uplift — high-maturity companies earn comparable margins and more than triple the three-year ROIC of less-mature peers.

    Technology alone doesn't drive performance; clear KPIs, disciplined resource allocation, and continuous performance management create the conditions for AI to pay off.

  • Productivity is the mechanism: AI delivers gains only when it moves beyond pilots into core operational processes across multiple functions.

    A global automaker saw software-development productivity rise up to 44%, incoming orders up ~40%, and identified 20-25% long-term efficiency potential as AI spread.

  • Plant-level case studies show large, concrete gains once the operating backbone is strengthened first.

    Siemens Nanjing: units/hour +50%, delivery lead time -83%. Qatar Shell: engineering costs -90%, upset response -98%, capex -64%. Midea: complaint resolution from 60 days to 1 day. CITIC Dicastal: labor productivity +27%, OEE +17%.

  • Leaders build the performance loop deliberately: start with a few high-value operational priorities, design early use cases for scale, build data/governance alongside the tech, embed capability where decisions are made, and sequence ambition to absorption capacity.

    Midsize companies (500-5,000 employees) are 'squeezed in the middle' — lagging on op-ex maturity without the scale advantages of the largest firms.

Section summaries

The setup: disruption, and two responses that mostly don't work alone

pp.1-2

Businesses feeling squeezed reach for evergreen cost-cutting and new digital/AI tools, but traditional cost transformations rarely sustain gains and most AI stays stuck in experimentation. A survey of 1,000 senior and midlevel executives frames how to respond.

AI and operational excellence increase productivity and returns

pp.3-5

Companies with AI embedded across functions earn nearly double the profit margins and 5x+ the three-year ROIC of peers with narrow AI. High operational-excellence maturity delivers a parallel uplift of more than triple ROIC — the two form a reinforcing performance loop.

Productivity is the link to results

pp.5-7

As operational-excellence maturity rises, productivity gains climb steadily and concentrate in the highest bands. Technology-enabled operating models — real-time data, digital workflows, advanced analytics — amplify the effect in a virtuous cycle.

AI pays off only beyond pilots

pp.7-9

Productivity improvement scales with the breadth of AI deployment; narrow use sees modest results. A global automaker's operating-model redesign delivered up to 44% software-dev productivity gains, ~40% more orders, and 20-25% efficiency potential.

What operational excellence means today

pp.9-11

It's the coherence of a company's operating system: cascading KPIs, enterprise measures tied to purpose, and disciplined real-time data use — treated as a living system of execution discipline, digital enablement, and continual learning (Exhibit 4). Service-oriented and advanced-manufacturing sectors score highest.

Case studies: strengthen the backbone, then scale AI

pp.11-17

Siemens Nanjing governed digital twins before scaling (units/hour +50%, lead time -83%). Qatar Shell prioritized asset reliability (engineering costs -90%, capex -64%). Midea rebuilt complaint resolution (60 days to 1 day; defects -43%). CITIC Dicastal automated core processes (labor productivity +27%).

How leaders build the loop deliberately

pp.17-20

Five moves: start with a few operational priorities; design for scale from the first use cases; build the operating backbone alongside the tech; embed capability where decisions are made; and sequence ambition with absorption capacity. Schneider's Evreux hit 38% circular product range and -43% Scope 3 in two years.
