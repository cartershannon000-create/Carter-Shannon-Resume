AI-to-EBITDA Strategy for PE-Backed SMB and Midmarket Companies

report · 2026-07-26 · 12 min read
private-equityaiebitdavalue-creationstrategy

TL;DR

PE firms don't have an AI awareness problem, they have an AI translation problem. This brief positions AI as a third value-creation lever alongside financial engineering and operational excellence, and lays out how to convert AI from scattered pilots and tool spend into measured EBITDA impact across portfolio companies through two engines, efficiency gains and opportunity gains, and a dual system-led plus user-led adoption model.

Key points

  • AI is becoming a third PE value-creation lever alongside financial engineering and operational excellence.

    The old model relied on leverage and multiple expansion; Bain frames the new deal math as '12 is the new 5,' forcing faster EBITDA growth.

  • Value comes from two engines: efficiency gains (do existing work faster/cheaper) and opportunity gains (open work that was previously uneconomic).

    Efficiency protects and expands margins without relying solely on headcount cuts or price increases.

  • Adoption requires both system-led (SaaS-like workflow integration) and user-led (Excel-like personal productivity) AI.

    System-led captures measurable EBITDA in core workflows; user-led drives adoption from the people closest to the work.

  • Conviction is high but operationalization is low: 84% of PE funds expect significant AI impact, yet only 7% have enterprise-scale deployment.

    A clear pilot-to-production gap where real alpha concentrates among disciplined executors.

  • AI in PE is not just cost takeout: 41% of initiatives target revenue growth versus 24% focused only on cost.

    Supports framing AI as both an efficiency and an opportunity lever, not a cost-cutting tool.

  • Useful modeling ranges: 63% of cost initiatives target 5-10% savings and 65% of revenue initiatives target 5-10% uplift.

    54-67% of use cases clear a 10% ROI hurdle when use-case selection and workflow design are disciplined.

Section summaries

Executive Thesis

sec.1

PE firms believe in AI but struggle to translate it into measurable EBITDA. The positioning is AI-to-EBITDA translation: identify where AI creates efficiency and opportunity, prioritize the highest-value portcos and workflows, build tailored systems, and enable user-led adoption.

Two Engines of AI Value

sec.2

Efficiency gains improve EBITDA by doing existing work faster, cheaper, and with fewer errors. Opportunity gains open capabilities and markets that were previously uneconomic for SMB and midmarket portcos.

Adoption Model: System-Led and User-Led

sec.3

System-led AI integrates into core workflows like SaaS and captures measurable EBITDA impact. User-led AI works like personal spreadsheet productivity and drives grassroots adoption. Both are required.

Strategic Frameworks

sec.4

Uses Deploy/Reshape/Invent, the McKinsey AI Value Creation Ladder, and a two-by-two adoption approach to sequence where and how portcos apply AI.

Evidence Bank

sec.5
